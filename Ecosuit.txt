# EcoSuit MK.I — Manufacturing Process
**Hemp-composite-core ceramic plate armor on an internal exoskeleton**

Scope: from feedstock to a flight-like MK.I article. Process is written as a build sequence, not a brochure. Flight certification (NASA-STD-6001 / 6016, ECSS, etc.) is a parallel track, not implied by these steps.

---

## 0. Process architecture (how the factory is organized)

| Cell | Output |
|---|---|
| A. Feedstock | Sized hemp fiber, ceramic slurry/powder, resins, metal/composite stock |
| B. Ceramic faces | Sintered, coated, densified plate faces |
| C. Hemp cores | Consolidated sandwich cores cut to segment net shape |
| D. Plate bonding | Finished armor segments (face + core + backing) |
| E. Exo frame | Lattice, yokes, longerons, joint housings |
| F. Joints & actuators | Bearings, convolutes, optional powered modules |
| G. Soft goods | Bladder, restraint, LCVG, seals, boots, gloves |
| H. Integration | Rear-entry HUT analog, hatch, helmet, PLSS interfaces |
| I. Finish & QA | Coatings, leak/proof, NDE, thermal/vacuum bake |

Rule: ceramic never sees primary structural load. Frame is made first as the master datum. Plates are made to the frame, not the other way around.

---

## 1. Incoming materials

### 1.1 Hemp
- Bast fiber, decorticated, degummed, sized for composite use.
- Forms: unidirectional tape, woven 2×2 / 4×4, and short-fiber mat for core fill.
- Incoming tests: moisture (< specified bake-out limit), fiber length distribution, residual pectin/oil, batch ID.
- Dry and bag immediately. Hemp is hygroscopic; treat it like prepreg, not like lumber.

### 1.2 Ceramic face stock
Two allowable routes (pick one per heat zone, not both on the same plate without a joint spec):

**Route S — Shuttle-derived silica RSI analog**
- High-purity amorphous silica fiber slurry (≥99% SiO₂ class).
- Cast, microwave or furnace sinter, machine to net.
- Density classes analogous to LI-900 (acreage) and LI-2200 / FRCI (high-load / high-abuse).

**Route A — Thin ceramic armor face**
- Alumina-silica or reaction-bonded ceramic sheet / tile, thinner than classic RSI, used where impact and abrasion dominate over pure insulation thickness.

### 1.3 Matrix / adhesives
- High-temp matrix for hemp core: phenolic, polyimide, or qualified ceramic-compatible resin. Epoxy only for ground demonstrators.
- Face-to-core and core-to-backing: high-temp flexible adhesive family (RTV-class silicone descendants or equivalent space-rated flexible bond). Bondline thickness controlled (<0.25 mm typical target, process-owned).
- All chemistries: outgassing, toxicity, flammability coupons before any manned article.

### 1.4 Frame stock
- S-glass / carbon hybrid prepreg, titanium or Al-Li fittings, 3D-printed high-temp thermoplastic tooling inserts (demonstrator) or machined metal (flight-like).
- Fasteners: captive, captive-retained, vacuum-compatible.

### 1.5 Coatings
- Black high-temp borosilicate / glass coating for HRSI-equivalent zones.
- White silica + alumina optical coating for LRSI-equivalent zones.
- Waterproofing / AO-resistant overcoat after densification.
- Visor: gold-amber thin-film stack on the slit optic only.

## 2. Master geometry

1. Scan or generate the anthropometric manikin (two sizes minimum: small / large).
2. Design the internal exo as the primary CAD master: spine, rib analog, pelvic yoke, scye, limb longerons, hatch ring.
3. Split the outer surface into **segments** (chest plastron, L/R pauldron, upper arm, lower arm, abdomen, L/R thigh, L/R shin, boots, helmet faceplate). Do not manufacture the original sketch as 10,000 loose triangles.
4. Apply the triangle facet pattern as **tooled surface language** on each segment (emboss, machine, or molded-in), not as separate parts.
5. Release: frame STEP + plate outer mold line + bond tool models + hole patterns. Frozen datum is the frame.

---

## 3. Ceramic face manufacture

### 3.1 Silica RSI analog (Route S)
1. Slurry mix: silica fiber + binder + water, solids content locked by recipe.
2. Cast into segment-shaped molds (not 6×6 squares). Thickness from heat map (thicker on ventral / re-entry analog zones).
3. Dry under controlled humidity so the block does not crack.
4. Sinter / microwave-fire to target density.
5. CNC or 5-axis grind the inner mold line (IML) flat and the outer mold line (OML) to the facet pattern.
6. **Densify the IML** (fill near-surface voids) so the bond does not see a weak, fluffy interface. This is the Shuttle lesson; do not skip it.
7. Coat OML (black or white per zone). Fire / cure the coating.
8. Waterproof / AO coat.
9. NDE: tap test, mass, thickness map, coating crack inspect, edge chip spec.

### 3.2 Thin ceramic face (Route A)
1. Press or cast thin ceramic blank.
2. Sinter.
3. Grind OML facets and IML.
4. Coat as above.
5. Same NDE.

Edge spec: chamfer or radius every free edge. Ceramic dies at sharp corners.

---

## 4. Hemp core manufacture

Goal: a core that is light, damps impact, isolates strain, and presents two bondable faces.

### 4.1 Ply book
- Typical demonstrator stack (adjust after coupon program):
  - Outer skins: woven hemp, 2–4 plies, fiber oriented to segment principal stress.
  - Core: hemp mat / oriented hurd / or hemp-fiber honeycomb analog.
  - Optional hybrid: one carbon or S-glass veil on the backing side for fastener bearing.
- Fiber volume fraction owned by process (research band around ~30–40 wt% hemp is a starting point, not a flight number).

### 4.2 Consolidation
1. Dry fiber: oven bake-out, then dry bay only.
2. Impregnate (prepreg or wet layup in sealed tool).
3. Lay into a matched tool that already has the segment curvature.
4. Vacuum bag + cure per matrix (phenolic needs its own ramp and post-cure).
5. Demold. Trim to net with diamond or carbide tooling; hemp abrades tools.
6. Face both sides (light skim) so bondlines are flat.
7. Dry again. Bag with desiccant until bonding.

### 4.3 Core coupons (gate, not optional)
Every batch: density, thickness, flatwise tension, flatwise compression, shear, moisture regain, TGA/DSC, outgassing. No plate bonding until the batch coupon book is signed.

---

## 5. Backing and hard points

1. Lay or machine the inner backing (thin composite sheet or metallic doubler).
2. Co-cure or secondarily bond inserts: threaded hard points, locator pins, shear clips.
3. Backing hole pattern is drilled to the **frame** drill jig, not to the ceramic.
4. Edge seal the hemp (resin dam or foil/tape closeout) so vacuum and moisture cannot wick the core.

---

## 6. Segment bonding (the SIP analog)

This is the Shuttle tile + Nomex pad + RTV stack, rewritten. 
Ceramic face  — flexible adhesive Hemp core     — flexible adhesive Backing       — mechanical + adhesive to exo frame

1. Plasma or approved abrasion of ceramic IML and core faces.
2. Apply controlled-thickness adhesive (stencil or film).
3. Assemble in a bonding fixture that holds OML contour and IML parallelism.
4. Vacuum bag or press. Cure per adhesive spec.
5. Post-bond inspect:
   - Bondline thickness map (UT or witness coupons)
   - Tap / thermography for voids
   - Edge fillet continuity
   - Mass
6. Proof the segment in flatwise tension at a process knockdown of design load. Fail the bond, not the ceramic, on purpose during process development.

**Do not** bond ceramic directly to the metal/composite frame. The hemp core is the strain isolator. That is the whole point.

---

## 7. Internal exoskeleton manufacture

### 7.1 Lattice and yokes
1. Cut / lay hybrid prepreg longerons and nodes, or machine Ti fittings and bond to composite tubes.
2. Cure in hard tools referenced to the anthropometric master.
3. Machine interface pads where segments land (flat, datumed).
4. Install captive fasteners and alignment pins.
5. Coat / anodize / paint metal; edge-seal composites.

### 7.2 Joint housings
- Shoulder, elbow, hip, knee, ankle: bearing seats and convolute attach rings machined as separate articles.
- Bearings: purchased space-rated or demonstrator equivalents, installed with specified preload and seal.
- Rolling convolutes: fabric/elastomer build on a mandrel, then trimmed to the housing.

### 7.3 Optional powered modules
- Actuator cans are separate LRUs. Wire/fluid pass-throughs are defined before first article, not after.
- Demonstrator may use industrial servos; flight-like article needs vacuum, thermal, and EMI design from day one.

### 7.4 Frame QA
- Laser scan vs CAD.
- Proof load the frame alone (no plates) in the design load cases: donning, 1g stand, fall, tool reaction, pressure (if the frame is part of the pressure path).
- Only then release the frame as the plate-mount datum.

---

## 8. Helmet

1. Faceplate: ceramic or ceramic-faced composite, same bond stack as body plates if heat-loaded; polycarbonate or similar only for the slit optic.
2. Inner pressure visor / HUD ring: the original sketch’s concentric/spiral geometry lives here, behind the slit.
3. Slit optic: bonded after faceplate coating. Gold-amber stack last.
4. Neck ring / bearing: machined, seals installed, leak-checked as a subassembly before it ever sees the HUT.

---

## 9. Soft goods and pressure vessel

Even an “Iron Man” hard suit still needs:

- Comfort layer
- Liquid cooling / ventilation garment
- Bladder (urethane-coated fabric or equivalent)
- Restraint layer
- Wrist and waist disconnects
- Gloves and boots as separate certified articles

Build these on existing EMU / xEMU / Mark III process lines where possible. Do not invent a new bladder process unless the pressure architecture demands it.

If the design is true hard-suit: HUT + brief are composite pressure vessels (S-glass or carbon, toughened epoxy or equivalent), rear hatch with dual seal, proof-tested before armor is hung.

If the design is hybrid MCP + gas: document which segments are pressure-critical and which are only thermal/MMOD.

---

## 10. Integration sequence (order matters)

1. Dress the manikin or subject stand-in with LCVG + bladder + restraint.
2. Drop on the exo frame. Index with shoulder harness / hip yoke. Torque frame joints to spec.
3. Hang armor segments from the inside out / top down:
   - Pelvic yoke and abdomen
   - Chest plastron
   - Pauldrons
   - Arms
   - Thighs / shins / boots
   - Helmet last
4. Each segment: pin → seat → torque → gap/step measure (Shuttle tile discipline, human-scale).
5. Fill gaps with qualified filler / overlapping scale seals.
6. Route cooling, comms, power through defined frame channels. No wires trapped under ceramic.
7. Install hatch, PLSS interfaces, relief valves, connectors.
8. Full-suit leak check at incremental pressure.
9. Proof pressure on the vessel (not on the ceramic).
10. Functional joint ROM on a stand, then on a person.

---

## 11. Finishing

- Touch-coat any coating holidays.
- Serial-mark every segment and every frame node. Genealogy is mandatory; Shuttle tile history is the warning.
- Photograph OML map (flat light + raking light) as the as-built baseline.
- Bag the suit with desiccant if it will sit. Hemp cores do not get to drink shop air.

---

## 12. Test gates (minimum before calling it MK.I)

| Gate | What | Pass sense |
|---|---|---|
| Coupon | Face / core / bond | Strength, thermal conductivity, outgassing |
| Segment | One chest + one pauldron | Impact, flatwise tension, thermal cycle |
| Joint | One live shoulder | ROM, leak, plate-to-plate clash |
| Frame | Bare exo | Proof load, scan vs CAD |
| Suit | Integrated article | Leak, proof, 1g mobility, thermal-vac bake, NDE after |

Thermal-vac: cycle the suit, then re-tap and re-scan every plate. Look for bond lift and coating craze. That is how tiles actually fail.

---

## 13. Repair doctrine (build it in, or you will invent it in the field)

- Segments are LRUs. Four fasteners + two pins, not a bonded-for-life tile field.
- Spare cores and spare faces stocked as matched sets.
- Field repair: swap segment. Depot repair: rebond core or recast face.
- Never grind a cracked ceramic face and fly it. Replace.

---

## 14. Demonstrator vs flight-like (do not confuse them)

**Ground MK.I demo (allowed shortcuts)**
- Epoxy hemp cores
- 3D-printed joint housings
- Off-the-shelf bearings
- Cosmetic ceramic coating on a non-RSI substrate

**Flight-like MK.I (no shortcuts)**
- Qualified matrix and adhesives
- Real RSI or thin ceramic faces with densified IML
- Space-rated bearings and seals
- Full outgassing / flammability / toxicity book
- Traceable fiber lots and cure charts

Build the demo so the *interfaces* are already flight-like (hole patterns, gap/step, rear hatch, segment splits). Change materials later. Do not change the architecture later.

---

## 15. Shop traveler (one-line sequence)

Hemp bake-out → core layup/cure → backing + inserts → ceramic cast/sinter/grind/coat/densify → face-core-backing bond → NDE + proof → frame layup/machine → joint install → frame proof → soft goods → frame on body → hang segments → gap/step → leak/proof → thermal-vac → serial book closeout.
